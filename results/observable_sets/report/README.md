# Observable sets under shot and gate noise: report figures

Generated by `../report_figures.py` from `../data/`. Each figure is saved as PNG and PDF, with the plotted numbers in a CSV
of the same name. `facts.md` has every number quoted below, plus the checks that have no figure.

## Common setup

- **Reservoir:** the CLI's TFIM (`--hamiltonian tfim`: random couplings J ∈ [0, 1], field 1, time 1, 2 Trotter
  steps).
- **Windows:** input reset on, window L = 10, seeds 42–51.
- **Shading:** mean ± s.e. over seeds.
- **Noise model:** depolarizing noise after every native gate, at the stated two-qubit fidelity. One-qubit error is a
  fifth of the two-qubit error, and readout and reset errors are 0.07%.
- **Current hardware:** silicon spin-qubit two-qubit fidelities are around 98–99%.
  - Quobly's slide quotes 98%, and its public material claims more than 99%.
  - The best published silicon results are 99.3–99.5%.
  - Read the 98% and 99% panels together as "current hardware".
- **Shot budget:** shots per window, split evenly over a set's measurement settings. "Exact" means exact expectation
  values.

## Figures

**Fig. 1: Memory capacity vs shot budget (n = 5 qubits).**
- With exact expectation values, measuring X and Y as well as Z raises memory capacity from 5.7 to 8.7 (noiseless).
- With finite shots, the gain needs a minimum budget, and that budget grows with gate noise. At 98%, Z-strings and
  XYZ-strings tie at 10³ shots per window (2.2 each). XYZ-strings leads at 10⁴ (3.8 vs 3.2) and at 10⁵ (5.6 vs 4.0).
- Nine settings (XYZ + 2-body) never beat three (XYZ-strings).

**Fig. 2: Gain of measuring X and Y as well as Z (XYZ-strings − Z-strings, paired over seeds).**

As fidelity improves, the curves move to lower shot budgets.

| Two-qubit fidelity | Memory: gain > 0 (beyond 1 s.e.) | Memory: gain ≥ 0.5 | Fraud AP, 10³ shots | Fraud AP, 10⁴ shots |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| noiseless | ~3·10² | ~10³ | +.005 (ns) | +.066 |
| 99.5% | ~10³ | ~3·10³ | — | — |
| 99% | ~10³ | ~3·10³ | −.015 (Z better on 10/10 seeds) | +.036 (XYZ better on 9/10) |
| 98% | ~3·10³ | ~10⁴ | −.019 (Z better on 10/10 seeds) | +.020 (XYZ better on 9/10) |

**Fig. 3: Fraud detection, test customer AP on scenario 3 (n = 4).**
- No finite budget reaches logistic regression on the raw window (0.332, dashed line).
- The best result at 10⁵ shots is XYZ-strings: 0.291 noiseless, 0.249 at 99%, 0.209 at 98%.
- With exact values, XYZ-strings matches logistic regression at every noise level (0.348 to 0.349). So gate noise
  costs only through the shot noise.
- At 98–99% fidelity and ≤ 10⁴ shots, plain local Z (4 features) is as good as any set.

**Fig. 4: Why this happens: feature spread across windows by Pauli weight (memory task, n = 5, XYZ-strings, exact).**
- Gate noise shrinks every feature, and shrinks high-weight correlators the most. At 98%, the spread drops 3.7× at
  weight 1 and 15× at weight 5.
- The dotted lines show the shot-noise standard error per feature at 10³, 10⁴ and 10⁵ shots per window (3
  settings). Once a feature falls below a line, it is lost at that budget.

## Facts for the text (no figure; see `facts.md`)

- **Readout error barely matters.** Going from 0.07% to 1% readout error at 98% fidelity changes memory capacity by
  at most 0.12 (mean 0.05).
- **Native bases don't matter.** Measuring Z after two fixed random single-qubit frames (`frames-3`) performs like
  XYZ-strings: memory capacity differs by at most 0.18. What matters is the number of settings, not whether the basis
  is native to the hardware.
- **Full tomography loses at realistic budgets.** With 81 settings (n = 4, 98%), it is the worst set up to 10⁴ shots
  (2.5 vs 3.8 for XYZ-strings). It only leads with exact values (9.1 vs 7.2).
- **A pulse-level model agrees.** SpinPulse, with 1/f dephasing calibrated to the same two-qubit fidelities (P3,
  n = 5), gives the same crossover: the gain turns positive from about 3·10³ shots at both 99% and 98%. There, the
  features shrink about equally at every Pauli weight instead of high weights first. One-qubit gates come out at
  99.998%, because SpinPulse only models dephasing.
